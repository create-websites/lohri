White House Easter Egg Roll: History and Traditions

Follow the evolution of the South Lawn tradition from 1878 to present day with music, egg races, souvenir eggs, and contemporary family activities.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterFamily, Community & Modern Traditions

Why the White House lawn became the setting

The tradition of egg rolling on Easter Monday was well-established in Washington before President Rutherford B. Hayes started the annual White House Easter Egg Roll in 1878, following Congress's restriction of public access to the U.S. Capitol grounds.

A tradition that kept evolving

Over time, the U.S. Marine Band joined the festivities, adding music, games, and public appearances to the event. Egg-roll races eventually became a popular activity during the nineteenth century.

Souvenir eggs and family programming

The Reagan administration launched a search for wooden eggs signed by famous individuals in 1981. These painted eggs became cherished mementos, leading to the expansion of programs to include storytelling, performances, and interactive stations.

Why the event matters beyond politics

The Egg Roll, a long-standing civic family tradition held at a historic political site, remains popular due to the charming concept of children coming together on the South Lawn for spring festivities. Despite facing obstacles such as war, renovations, inclement weather, and public-health crises, the event has persevered.

Frequently asked questions

When was the first annual White House Easter Egg Roll?

According to the White House Historical Association, the first White House Easter Egg Roll took place on April 22, 1878.

Who opened the White House grounds to egg rollers?

In 1878, President Rutherford B. Hayes allowed children to access the grounds.
