Zero-Waste Easter Baskets: Practical Eco-Friendly Ideas

Create Easter baskets using reusable containers, practical gifts, and compostable filler instead of disposable plastic.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterCrafts, Decor & DIY

Start with the container

Repurpose a basket, storage bin, tote, garden pot, or fabric bag that can be used beyond Easter. By reusing the container annually, you can make a significant impact.

Replace plastic grass

Recycled shredded paper, a fabric scarf, cloth napkins, or crinkle-cut kraft paper can be used as cushioning for gifts, avoiding the need for plastic packaging. Using fabric also makes it easier to unpack gifts around pets and toddlers.

Choose gifts that get used

Instead of novelty plastic, consider replacing with books, art supplies, seed packets, sidewalk chalk, reusable water bottles, gardening tools, and experience coupons. When buying treats, opt for favorites rather than a large assortment that may not be eaten.

Reuse the decor next year

Keep ribbons, wooden eggs, fabric bunting, and sturdy baskets organized together post-holiday in a labeled Easter box to prevent the temptation of purchasing new decorations each spring.

Frequently asked questions

What can replace plastic Easter grass?

Shredded paper, fabric scraps, cloth napkins, or reusable scarves are all effective options.

Can a zero-waste basket still include candy?

Yes, the aim is to reduce waste, not strive for perfection. Select treats that people will truly appreciate and minimize excess packaging whenever possible.
