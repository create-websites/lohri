Holy Week Explained: From Palm Sunday to Easter

A comprehensive overview of Palm Sunday, Maundy Thursday, Good Friday, Holy Saturday, and Easter Sunday.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterHistory, Folklore & Spiritual Origins

Palm Sunday: entry into Jerusalem

Palm Sunday marks the beginning of Holy Week as we remember Jesus' triumphant entry into Jerusalem and the crowds that greeted him. Some churches incorporate palm branches in their worship services, while others use local greenery in areas where palms are not readily available.

Maundy Thursday: meal, service and humility

Maundy Thursday commemorates the Last Supper and, in various customs, Jesus washing his disciples' feet. Activities may involve partaking in communion, foot washing, removing items from the altar, or observing a peaceful vigil late into the evening.

Good Friday and Holy Saturday

Good Friday is a solemn day that centers on the crucifixion, with traditions such as readings of the Passion, veneration of the cross, and extended prayer. Holy Saturday is a day of waiting that often culminates in the Easter Vigil after dark in many churches.

Easter Sunday: resurrection and joy

On Easter Sunday, the resurrection is celebrated through music, flowers, light, and communal worship. Sunrise services, cathedral liturgies, and intimate local gatherings all symbolize the transition from sorrow to optimism.

When attending a new church, be sure to review service times and dress codes; traditions for Holy Week differ greatly among different denominations and cultures.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Good Friday and Easter Sunday?

Good Friday commemorates the crucifixion; Easter Sunday celebrates the resurrection.

Why is Holy Week called holy?

It condenses the key moments of the Christian Passion story into a series of days filled with prayer and worship.
