Easter Ham vs. Lamb: Choosing the Holiday Centerpiece

Evaluate two traditional Easter centerpieces based on their taste, meaning, preparation method, guest preferences, and leftovers.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterCulinary & Baking Trends

Why families choose ham

Ham is a common choice for North American Easter dinners as it can serve a large group, can be made in advance, and pairs well with both sweet and tangy sauces. Leftover bone-in ham is also versatile for making sandwiches, soups, and breakfast meals.

Why families choose lamb

Lamb holds significant symbolic and culinary importance during Passover and Easter in various traditions. Roasted leg or shoulder of lamb pairs beautifully with garlic, rosemary, lemon, and herbs, while chops are a great option for smaller families.

Choose based on your table, not a rule

Consider guest preferences, oven capacity, budget constraints, and desired side dishes when planning your meal. Opting for a variety of dishes on the table, such as a smaller roast and a vegetarian main course, is a great way to accommodate different tastes and dietary restrictions.

Plan leftovers before you cook

Leftover ham can be transformed into biscuits, strata, or bean soup, while leftover lamb can be repurposed into sandwiches, grain bowls, or shepherd's pie-style bakes. It is crucial to promptly cool and refrigerate leftovers rather than trying to stretch them into one more meal.

Frequently asked questions

Is lamb more traditional than ham for Easter?

The symbolism of lamb has a long history in religious contexts, while ham has become closely linked with Easter in numerous North American homes. Both hold strong traditions.

Which is easier for a large group?

Ham is usually easier to prepare since it requires less last-minute cooking when heated and glazed.
