Non-Candy Easter Basket Ideas for Kids

Include books, outdoor toys, art supplies, experiences, and practical gifts in Easter baskets instead of just sugary treats.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterFamily, Community & Modern Traditions

Build around a child’s real interests

Choose one interest - such as drawing, sports, reading, gardening, music, science, or pretend play - and gather a few small items that reflect the child rather than a generic Easter aisle to make the basket feel more thoughtful.

Add an experience instead of more objects

Add a coupon for a museum visit, picnic, baking day, mini-golf outing, or a family movie night of your choice. These experiences require no storage space and can make the basket feel more special without any added clutter.

Use books and creative supplies

Picture books, graphic novels, puzzles, sidewalk chalk, watercolor sets, stickers, and craft kits are suitable for a wide range of ages. When selecting items for toddlers, opt for materials that are age-appropriate and free of small choking hazards.

Keep candy optional, not forbidden

You don't have to turn a non-candy basket into a lecture on sweets. Including one favorite treat with practical gifts can still greatly cut down on the amount of candy.

Frequently asked questions

What are good non-candy basket fillers for young children?

When selecting items for children, consider board books, bath toys, chunky crayons, bubbles, and outdoor playthings that are suitable for their age.

Can I mix candy and non-candy gifts?

Certainly. Including a small treat along with books or activities can help create a well-rounded basket.
