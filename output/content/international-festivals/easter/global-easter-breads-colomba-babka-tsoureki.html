Global Easter Breads: Colomba, Babka and Tsoureki

Explore the cultural significance of three festive breads: Italian colomba, Polish babka, and Greek tsoureki.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterCulinary & Baking Trends

Colomba di Pasqua: the dove-shaped Italian bread

Colomba is a decadent Italian Easter bread that resembles a dove, adorned with almonds and a crispy sugar topping. Though it shares similarities with panettone in its enriched dough, Colomba has a unique seasonal essence all its own.

Polish babka: tall, rich and celebratory

Babka comes in various variations throughout Eastern Europe, with Polish Easter versions typically being tall, buttery cakes or yeast breads infused with citrus, vanilla, or raisins and topped with glaze or sugar.

Greek tsoureki: aromatic braided bread

Tsoureki, a fragrant braided bread popular during Greek Easter, is infused with aromatic ingredients like mahlepi and sometimes mastic. It is often adorned with red-dyed eggs, adding a festive touch to the bread.

Bake the tradition, not just the shape

Every type of bread is deeply rooted in a specific cultural and religious background. When trying a new bread, look for recipes from chefs who share the regional ingredients and family traditions rather than solely relying on its visual appeal.

Frequently asked questions

Are these breads interchangeable?

They differ in doughs, scents, forms, and cultural backgrounds, despite their common appearance during Easter.

Why are eggs sometimes baked into tsoureki?

Red eggs are frequently seen as a traditional Greek Easter symbol and can be used decoratively in the braided loaf.
