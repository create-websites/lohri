Thanksgiving 1621: Myths, Reality and Wampanoag Perspectives

Distinguish the well-known Thanksgiving tale from the 1621 harvest event, Wampanoag negotiations, and Indigenous viewpoints that challenge the narrative.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingHistory, Heritage & Perspectives

What the surviving record actually describes

The "first Thanksgiving" was a three-day harvest celebration in Plymouth in 1621, attended by English colonists, Ousamequin (Massasoit), and around ninety Wampanoag men. The English account of the event is concise, mentioning fowling, games, and the Wampanoag bringing five deer, but does not align with the idealized image often depicted in modern culture.

Referring to the event as the first Thanksgiving may overshadow the long-standing gratitude ceremonies of the Wampanoag communities and the pre-1621 days of thanksgiving and prayer observed by English communities. The modern U.S. holiday did not develop until much later.

Alliance, survival and political context

The meal took place in the context of a political alliance, rather than in isolation. Ousamequin had formed a pact with the Plymouth colonists for mutual protection. Wampanoag support, which included sharing agricultural expertise and engaging in diplomacy, was crucial for the colony's survival. At the same time, the alliance was beneficial for Wampanoag strategic goals in a shifting regional environment.

The brief collaboration should not overshadow the lasting impact of English settlement, land loss, conflict, and upheaval for Native communities. A thoughtful Thanksgiving narrative must recognize both the initial meeting in 1621 and the ongoing effects of colonization.

How the national story was built

The 1621 gathering did not become an annual national holiday right away. Instead, it was later romanticized by 19th-century writers and editors as the origin story of the United States. Over time, Thanksgiving proclamations and family traditions shaped the holiday into its modern form.

In today's families and classrooms, the best approach is not to swap one oversimplified narrative for another. It is to differentiate between evidence, later myths, and current Indigenous perspectives—and to acknowledge that Native nations are still vibrant, varied, and modern.

A better way to talk about Thanksgiving

Utilize names like Wampanoag instead of general terms and steer clear of stereotypical portrayals in costumes and classroom exercises. Provide a detailed account of who attended the 1621 event, accurately depict information from sources, and acknowledge the varying interpretations of the event among different communities.

Frequently asked questions

Was the 1621 gathering really the first Thanksgiving?

It can be more accurately described as a 1621 harvest celebration, with both Indigenous peoples and Europeans having their own traditions of gratitude, while the modern U.S. Thanksgiving holiday emerged at a later time.

Were the Wampanoag simply invited dinner guests?

The political, strategic, and social dynamics of the historical relationship were evident as Ousamequin and the Plymouth colonists forged an alliance, with Wampanoag members contributing deer to the gathering.
