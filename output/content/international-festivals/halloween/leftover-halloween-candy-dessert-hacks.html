The day after Halloween can bring a unique pantry dilemma: an abundance of small candies with different flavors. Think of them as mix-ins for baking. Start by organizing them by texture and taste, then mix together candies that are alike instead of tossing them all into one dish.

Chop chocolate into baking mix-ins

You can swap mini chocolate bars for chocolate chips in cookies, blondies, or brownies. For easier cutting and less smearing, freeze soft or caramel-filled pieces briefly before chopping.

Build bark and snack mixes

When melting chocolate, be sure to spread it thinly and sprinkle chopped candy, pretzels, or nuts before it hardens. Popcorn blends nicely with small candies to maintain a perfect balance of sweet and salty flavors.

Use candies as toppings, not just ingredients

Crushed candies are versatile toppings for cupcakes, ice cream, milkshakes, or a basic sheet cake. Sprinkling them on top allows for easy identification of ingredients and helps to avoid allergen contamination.

Store what you cannot use immediately

Several types of candies can be stored according to package instructions, while certain baked goods can be successfully frozen. It is best to discard any unwrapped, damaged, or questionable treats instead of attempting to salvage them in a recipe.

Frequently asked questions

Can leftover candy be frozen?

Certain candies may freeze more successfully than others. It is recommended to consult the manufacturer’s instructions, when possible, and anticipate alterations in texture for candies containing caramel, wafers, or fillings.

What is the easiest leftover candy recipe?

Chocolate bark and cookie mix-ins are easy to make and allow you to mix multiple candies together with minimal preparation.
