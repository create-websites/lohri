The Presidential Turkey Pardon: History, Myth and Media Ritual

Investigate the history of the White House turkey pardon, distinguishing between its traditional roots and the modern-day annual event that captivates the media.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingHistory, Heritage & Perspectives

The origin stories are messier than the joke

Tales of presidents pardoning turkeys date back long before the current ceremony, though not all were official Thanksgiving "pardons." The White House Historical Association recounts a tale of Abraham Lincoln's son Tad convincing him to spare a Christmas turkey, with subsequent presidents occasionally choosing to let presentation birds survive.

These episodes should be seen as forerunners rather than part of a single unbroken formal tradition.

When the modern pardon took shape

Turkey presentations to presidents were a common occurrence in the twentieth century, with John F. Kennedy famously sparing a turkey in 1963 and Ronald Reagan adopting pardon language in the 1980s.

The tradition of granting a presidential pardon to a turkey is believed to have begun in 1989, when President George H. W. Bush first pardoned a turkey. Subsequent presidents have upheld this playful pre-Thanksgiving ritual.

A ritual of media, agriculture and humor

The event combines various American traditions, including the presidency, agricultural organizations, holiday TV specials, and political satire. Speakers often incorporate humor and clever wordplay, with the turkey serving as a playful element in the midst of a hectic political climate.

The "pardon" should be viewed as symbolic language, as it is a ceremonial gesture rather than a constitutional act of clemency.

How to discuss the tradition neutrally

The event can be examined without viewing it as either significant diplomacy or trivial show. It is a regular tradition at the White House, stemming from turkey ceremonies, changing presidential practices, and the public's interest in seasonal customs.

Frequently asked questions

Which president made the turkey pardon an annual tradition?

The current yearly custom is typically credited to President George H. W. Bush in 1989.

Is a turkey pardon a real legal pardon?

It is a symbolic ritual, not a pardon for a criminal offense under the constitution.
