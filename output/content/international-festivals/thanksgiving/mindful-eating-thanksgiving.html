Mindful Eating at the Thanksgiving Table

Savor your Thanksgiving meal at a leisurely pace, focusing on mindfulness and portion control rather than strict dieting rules, to fully enjoy the experience.

Arrive hungry, not ravenous

Not eating meals in order to "save calories" can lead to difficulties recognizing fullness later on. Having a regular breakfast or a light lunch can prepare you to fully enjoy dinner, rather than feeling rushed to eat.

Survey before serving

Take a good look at all the food options before selecting your favorites, including those that hold sentimental value, and serve yourself small portions initially. Feel free to come back for seconds if you'd like.

Slow the first ten minutes

It's easy to rush through the first moments of a holiday meal. Take breaks from eating, engage in conversation, pay attention to the different sensations, and allow the meal to progress naturally. Slowing down the pace of eating can enhance comfort.

Avoid moral labels for food

Thanksgiving does not require categorizing foods as "good," "bad," "clean," or "cheat." Food can offer nourishment, enjoyment, tradition, and memories all at once. If you have specific dietary restrictions, follow your healthcare provider's guidance. Otherwise, being flexible is often more sustainable than feeling guilty.

Frequently asked questions

Should I skip breakfast before Thanksgiving dinner?

Eating a balanced meal earlier in the day can help people approach dinner without feeling overly hungry.

What if I eat more than planned?

There is no need to punish or compensate for one big meal. Just return to your regular routine and prioritize comfort, hydration, and overall well-being.
