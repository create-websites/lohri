Inclusivity in Modern Durga Puja Pandals

As Durga Puja communities reconsider participation, they are exploring women-led priest teams, inclusive pandal design, and social-awareness themes.

Durga Puja Hub
Why this guide matters

A diverse group of individuals attend a public festival. Inclusion examines if both devotion and cultural engagement are available to all, not just symbolically but also physically and socially.

Women-led priest teams

One example of change highlighted in your brief is women-led priest teams. Their presence challenges assumptions about ritual authority and sparks discussions on training, tradition, and community acceptance.

What matters is not just novelty, but continued involvement and skill.

Accessible pandal design

The inclusion of ramps, improved pathways, seating, clear signage, and crowd assistance can greatly enhance the visit for elderly guests and individuals with disabilities.

It is most effective to design accessibility from the start rather than adding it as an afterthought once the project is finished.

Social awareness through themes

Certain modern pandals utilize artwork to tackle societal issues, transforming the festival into a platform for public discourse. However, the credibility of a theme is enhanced when organizers effectively translate the message into action.

For example, an accessibility-themed pandal should itself be accessible.

Inclusion without losing sacred focus

Durga Puja can increase involvement without losing its sacred essence. Defined ceremonial areas, respectful understanding, and intentional planning facilitate diverse groups in coexisting peacefully.

Inclusion is achieved when individuals feel accepted without diminishing the importance of the sacred core.
