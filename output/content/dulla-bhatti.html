Dulla Bhatti
Robin Hood of Punjab

The Hero of Lohri

Lohri's cultural soul is deeply intertwined with the legend of Dulla Bhatti, a 16th-century folk hero whose story of justice and compassion echoes through the festival's songs.

More Than a Myth

Dulla Bhatti was a real figure who lived during the reign of Emperor Akbar. He rebelled against unjust taxes and became a champion of the poor, earning him a permanent place in Punjabi folklore.

Protector

Famous for saving young girls from slavery and arranging their marriages with dignity.

Rebel

Led a revolt against the exploitative land revenue system imposed on farmers.

Icon

His legacy is preserved in the songs sung by children door-to-door on Lohri.

Historical Context

Who Was Dulla Bhatti?

The Rebel

Living during Akbar's reign, Dulla Bhatti challenged imperial authority. He refused to pay unjust taxes and effectively redistributed wealth from oppressive officials to the poor villagers.

The Outlaw

Branded a criminal by the state but a hero by the masses, he lived as an outlaw to protect his people's rights, becoming a symbol of resistance against tyranny.

A Moral Icon

"His defiance made him a hero among the masses... acting as a father figure to the helpless."

16th Century Punjab

The Famous Tale

Daughters of Punjab

The Rescue

Sundri and Mundri

Two orphaned girls were in danger of being sold into slavery by a powerful landlord. Dulla Bhatti intervened, rescuing them and taking them under his protection as his own daughters.

Immortalized in "Sundar Mundariye Ho"

The Wedding

He arranged their marriages on Lohri night. Since they were poor, he provided a simple dowry himself:

  • A Ser (measure) of Sugar
  • Sesame Seeds (Til)

This act of compassion elevated him from a rebel to a moral icon.

Enduring Spirit

A Living Legend

Oral History

Songs passed down through generations serve as collective memory, ensuring his story isn't confined to books but lives in the hearts of people.

The Bonfire

The communal fire mirrors his spirit—bringing people together, providing warmth, and symbolizing hope against harsh conditions.

Values

By remembering him, communities reaffirm timeless values: standing up against oppression and protecting the vulnerable.

"It is this blend of legend and festival that makes Lohri not just a seasonal celebration, but a cultural legacy."
