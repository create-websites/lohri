The Friendsgiving Hosting Playbook

Organize a stress-free Friendsgiving with assigned potluck dishes, dietary accommodations, beverage and dessert stations, seating arrangements, and easy cleanup strategies.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingHosting, Prep & Decor

Decide what Friendsgiving means for your group

Friendsgiving can take many forms - from a formal sit-down dinner or buffet to a casual potluck or brunch. Make sure to specify the format clearly in the invitation so guests know what to bring - whether it's a finished dish, ingredients, drinks, or just themselves.

Use categories instead of micromanaging recipes

Encourage a variety of mains, vegetables, starches, salads, desserts, and drinks to keep the potluck diverse and avoid an overload of sweets.

Inquire about food allergies and dietary preferences beforehand. Clearly mark dishes on the table to avoid guests having to question each chef.

Create stations that reduce kitchen traffic

Place beverages outside of the kitchen space. Designate a separate area for desserts or coffee to be served after the meal. Arrange plates and utensils at the start of the serving line and have napkins available at either end.

Make cleanup part of the design

Make sure to have empty containers on hand for storing leftovers and designate specific areas for trash, recycling, and dirty dishes. Consider assigning a few members of the group to a quick 15-minute cleanup session to help the host avoid being left with all the post-event mess.

Frequently asked questions

Should the host cook the turkey?

Friendsgiving is most successful when the host offers to provide one main dish and guests contribute to the rest of the menu, but only if the host is willing to do so.

How do I handle dietary restrictions?

Gather them in advance, clearly mark the dishes, and make sure each guest has multiple substantial choices.
