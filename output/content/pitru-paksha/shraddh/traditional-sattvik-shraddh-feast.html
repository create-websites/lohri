Traditional Sattvik Shraddh Feast: Kheer, Poori, Arbi, Pumpkin and More

Discover traditional sattvik dishes served during Shraddh, such as kheer, poori, arbi, and pumpkin, along with insights on regional differences and easy meal preparation tips.

Shraddh Hub
Why this guide matters

Shraddh meals are typically straightforward, sattvik, and influenced by family customs. North Indian households often serve dishes like kheer, poori, arbi, and pumpkin, while other regions incorporate their own unique grains, vegetables, sweets, and dietary guidelines.

Why sattvik food is emphasized

A sattvik meal helps maintain a calm atmosphere of traditional reverence by typically omitting alcohol and meat, and sometimes avoiding onion and garlic in households with this belief.

The goal is not to deprive oneself of food, but to maintain clarity, simplicity, and consistency within the sacred setting.

A classic North Indian menu

A typical meal might consist of poori, arbi cooked with light spices, pumpkin sabzi, fresh vegetables, dal, rice, and kheer. Traditional dishes loved by past generations are also made by families, as long as they adhere to household customs.

There is no single pan-Indian Shraddh menu.

Cook with ritual and food safety in mind

When cooking, ensure the kitchen is clean, portion quantities thoughtfully, and refrigerate dairy items as necessary. Prioritize modern food-safety practices when sharing meals with guests, priests, or community members.

Respect for ritual never requires ignoring hygiene.

Food as memory

Recipes can hold a richer family history than official records, with a grandmother's kheer recipe or a grandfather's favorite vegetable stirring up vivid memories.

Make sure to jot down these recipes during Pitru Paksha to turn the meal into both a tribute and a keepsake.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Is there one required Shraddh menu?

No. Menus vary by region, caste, family and local custom.

Are onion and garlic always prohibited?

Several families steer clear of them for sattvik meals, with variations in household customs.

Can a favorite family recipe be included?

Frequently, it is approved as long as it aligns with the family's dietary customs, as it can enhance the significance of the communal feast.
