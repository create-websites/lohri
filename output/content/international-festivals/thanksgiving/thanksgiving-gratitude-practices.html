Thanksgiving Gratitude Practices Beyond “What Are You Thankful For?”

Enhance the significance of Thanksgiving gratitude by incorporating story prompts, written notes, intergenerational questions, and practices that steer clear of artificial positivity.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingGratitude, Wellness & Traditions

Move from lists to stories

Instead of one-word answers, can you share a story for each of these questions: Who supported you this year? What everyday moment do you want to cherish? What valuable lesson did you gain from someone around this table?

Make room for complicated years

Expressing gratitude does not mean ignoring challenges. It is possible for a family to recognize difficulties like grief, sickness, career shifts, or disagreements, all while appreciating kindness, beauty, and moments of stability. This approach allows for a more genuine and authentic expression of gratitude.

Use written gratitude for quieter guests

Place cards have a dual purpose as note cards, allowing guests to express gratitude, share memories, or jot down thoughts they wish to keep with them. Children can opt to draw instead of writing.

Turn gratitude into action

One valuable question to consider is: How can we put our gratitude into action? This could involve reaching out to those in need, backing a local cause, passing down a cherished recipe, or making time for family before time slips away.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make gratitude activities less awkward?

Make sure they are optional, concise, and precise. Story prompts tend to flow more naturally than forcing everyone to give a formal speech.

Is gratitude appropriate during a hard year?

Gratitude and grief can coexist as long as hardship is not denied.
