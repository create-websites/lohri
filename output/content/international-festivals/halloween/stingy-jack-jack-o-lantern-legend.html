The jack-o’-lantern has become synonymous with Halloween, but its origins can be traced back to Irish folklore, where tales of a mischievous character named Stingy Jack were connected to mysterious lights and the tradition of carved lanterns that eventually made their way to America from across the Atlantic.

Who was Stingy Jack?

There are various versions of the folklore, but they all depict Jack as a cunning and selfish trickster who uses his wit to outsmart the Devil. In death, he is doomed to roam aimlessly with only a glowing coal as his guide. Since the story is passed down orally, there is no definitive version.

From wandering lights to carved faces

Throughout Britain and Ireland, eerie lights spotted above marshes were occasionally attributed to folklore about restless souls. Locals crafted lanterns out of turnips, mangel-wurzels, or other root veggies, carving them into ghoulish faces for autumn festivities. These grotesque visages served as a mix of playful, protective, and theatrical elements during seasonal get-togethers.

Why pumpkins took over

In North America, immigrants found pumpkins that were large, plentiful, and easier to carve. The pumpkin lantern quickly became both practical and visually striking. By the late 19th and early 20th centuries, illustrated magazines, postcards, and community events helped establish the orange jack-o’-lantern as a Halloween symbol.

A tradition that keeps reinventing itself

Contemporary carving can be as basic as triangle faces or as intricate as detailed portraits and sculptural designs. The age-old tradition continues to incorporate familiar elements: fresh harvest, a flickering light, a face, shadows, and a narrative meant to be passed down.

Frequently asked questions

Was Stingy Jack a historical person?

He should be regarded as a figure of folklore. Various renditions of the tale were passed down through word of mouth and evolved over the years.

Why are jack-o’-lanterns carved with scary faces?

Faces can draw inspiration from traditional masking and lantern practices, resulting in a diverse range of modern designs that span from eerie to humorous and ornamental.
