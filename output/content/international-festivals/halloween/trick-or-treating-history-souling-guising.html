Trick-or-Treating Through the Ages: Souling, Guising and Candy Runs

Trick-or-treating today may seem straightforward - don a costume, visit a door, and gather treats - but its origins are far more intriguing. It blends traditional door-to-door practices with community initiatives to transform Halloween antics into a organized family tradition.

Souling and seasonal visiting

During medieval and early modern Europe, it was common for less fortunate individuals and children to go from door to door during All Souls’ observances, offering prayers or songs in exchange for food. These traditions differed depending on the region and should not be viewed as a direct precursor, but they do highlight the long-standing practice of ritual visits during the season.

Guising brings performance to the doorstep

In Scotland and Ireland, individuals known as "guisers" would don disguises and entertain with songs, jokes, recitations, or tricks in exchange for food or coins. This focus on performance is why costumes and doorstep interactions became integral to Halloween traditions.

The North American neighborhood ritual

As Halloween gained popularity in the United States and Canada, communities began hosting parties, parades, and activities for children. The twentieth century, particularly after World War II, saw the rise of affordable candy marketing, suburban growth, and the establishment of the traditional trick-or-treating tradition we know today.

Making the tradition more inclusive

Numerous neighborhoods have adopted allergy-conscious treats, non-food alternatives, sensory-friendly hours, and "trunk-or-treat" gatherings. By incorporating these modern practices, the traditional act of neighbors welcoming children at their doors is maintained, ensuring a safer and more inclusive experience for all families.

Frequently asked questions

When did “trick or treat” become a common phrase?

The saying gained more prominence in North American newspapers and popular culture in the early 1900s, and saw significant growth in the postwar period.

Is trick-or-treating the same everywhere?

Differences in timing, age norms, candy traditions, and local regulations can greatly differ depending on the country, city, or neighborhood.
