Why Apta Leaves Are Exchanged as “Gold” on Dussehra

Discover the significance of exchanging Apta leaves as symbolic "gold" on Dussehra, particularly in Maharashtra and nearby traditions, by exploring their stories and social importance.

Dussehra Hub
Why this guide matters

The tradition transforms a simple leaf into a ceremonial offering, allowing prosperity to be shared symbolically rather than solely quantified by wealth.

A leaf that becomes “sona”

During Dussehra in western India, people swap Apta tree leaves, referring to them as symbolic gold or sona, expressing wishes for prosperity, goodwill, and success.

The tradition is unforgettable as it offers abundance in a simple way: a single leaf can symbolize a valuable present.

Legends of generosity

The custom is often depicted in popular stories as being rooted in acts of generosity and the sharing of wealth. While each version may vary, they all emphasize the same moral concept: true prosperity is found in the circulation of wealth, not in its accumulation.

This is why exchanges frequently occur between family members, neighbors, coworkers, and friends.

A social ritual of reconciliation

Celebrating festivals provides an opportunity to bridge the gap caused by distance or disagreements. Giving a token of gold can serve as a gesture of respect and reconciliation.

The value is relational, not financial.

Celebrate without harming trees

By adhering to the tradition, only take what is necessary and refrain from harming trees by stripping branches. When plant access is restricted, communities can utilize fallen leaves, shared bundles, or symbolic reusable cards.

The essence of abundance should not be gained at the cost of the living tree embodying the symbolism.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Why are Apta leaves called gold?

They are exchanged as symbolic sona, representing prosperity and goodwill.

Is this custom practiced across India?

It is commonly linked to Maharashtra and certain nearby regional customs.

Can the tradition be made eco-friendly?

Yes. Utilize small amounts, fallen leaves when suitable, and refrain from harming trees.
