Stress-Free Easter Hosting: A Simple Timeline

Create a practical Easter menu with a manageable preparation schedule, dishes that can be made in advance, a versatile table setup, and a more relaxed approach to hosting.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterFamily, Community & Modern Traditions

One week before: decide the shape of the day

Decide between brunch, lunch, or dinner, confirm the number of guests, inquire about dietary restrictions, and choose between plated, buffet-style, or family-style meals. This choice will impact future decisions.

Two to three days before: do the invisible work

Go shopping, clean serving pieces, prepare dressings and sauces, bake hearty desserts, and set aside drinks. Also, get table decor ready that doesn't include fresh flowers or food.

The day before: reduce oven traffic

Prepare casseroles, dice vegetables, arrange the table, and clear a spot in the fridge for the holiday feast. Create a brief oven plan to ensure the main course and sides are cooked at different temperatures to avoid overcrowding.

Easter day: protect a margin

Try to get the kitchen in order before guests show up. Set out a simple snack, ask guests to assist with drinks, and be okay with one dish not looking as good as you hoped. A calm host typically enhances the event more than a flawless centerpiece.

Frequently asked questions

What should I make ahead for Easter?

You can usually prepare desserts, dressings, cold salads, casseroles, and many side-dish components a day or two in advance.

Is brunch easier than dinner?

It can be done, especially with make-ahead bakes, fruit, and simple breads, but the optimal choice varies based on your schedule and guests.
