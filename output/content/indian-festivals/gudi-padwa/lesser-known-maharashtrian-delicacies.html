Beyond Shrikhand: Maharashtrian Festive Delicacies

Enhance your New Year spread with traditional Maharashtrian dishes like amti, usal, koshimbir, ghavan, basundi, and a variety

Gudi Padwa Hub
Why this guide matters

Maharashtrian New Year cuisine blends tradition, hospitality, and local flavors. Explore from the famous puran poli to a diverse spread of lesser-known dishes and portable gifts.

Go beyond the two famous sweets

While Puran poli and shrikhand are popular choices for Gudi Padwa menus, Maharashtrian festive cooking encompasses a wide range of dishes. Regional cuisines vary across Konkan, Desh, Vidarbha, Marathwada, and urban family traditions.

Creating a menu focused on more obscure dishes can add a personalized touch to the event and prevent it from becoming monotonous.

Amti, varan and the dal tradition

A plain varan with rice provides solace, while amti offers a complex sweet-sour-spicy flavor profile based on family preferences. These dishes can both complement a thali and offset sweeter desserts.

Instead of searching for the perfect “official” recipe, stick to the dal your family usually enjoys on special occasions.

Usal and sprouted dishes

Usal made with sprouted legumes brings a satisfying crunch, protein, and rich flavor. Different regions and personal taste can determine whether matki, moong, or other legumes are used.

A small serving of vegetables can be particularly beneficial if the rest of the meal is full of processed grains or sugary treats.

Koshimbir and fresh contrast

Koshimbir, a mixture of vegetables, yogurt, and peanuts among other ingredients, brings a refreshing crunch to the thali, subtly enhancing the overall meal.

Select a version that can be put together shortly before serving to ensure freshness.

Konkan and regional options

In coastal homes, it is common to find rice-based breads or pancakes, coconut-heavy dishes, and seasonal fruits and vegetables, rather than the typical "festival menu" found in generic cookbooks. In different areas, meals may be influenced by gram flour, peanuts, sesame, sorghum, or locally sourced vegetables.

The diversity in this region is a source of strength. The flavors of Gudi Padwa dishes should reflect the unique traditions and people of the area.

Desserts beyond shrikhand

When planning a festive meal, consider including basundi, kheer, fruit-based sweets, laddus, or homemade barfi. If you typically serve puran poli, opt for a lighter second sweet to balance the richness of the meal.

The most memorable meal often has contrast, not maximum quantity.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Is there one fixed Gudi Padwa menu across Maharashtra?

Menus differ based on the region, family customs, dietary choices, and ingredient availability.

What savory dish pairs well with puran poli?

A basic dal, amti, potato bhaji, usal, koshimbir, or seasonal vegetable can help offset the sweetness.

Can coastal Maharashtrian dishes be part of Gudi Padwa?

Indeed, festival cooking includes traditional regional family dishes.
