Celebrating Thanksgiving Abroad: An Expat Guide

Experience Thanksgiving overseas with adaptable ingredient swaps, new local friends, cozy kitchens, and customs centered on togetherness over perfect replicas.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingGratitude, Wellness & Traditions

Define what matters most to you

If common ingredients are too pricey or hard to find, choose a couple of key traditions that capture the essence of Thanksgiving. This could include turkey, cranberry sauce, pumpkin pie, watching football, practicing gratitude, or simply coming together at the dinner table.

Substitute by function, not by exact ingredient

If turkey is not an option, consider a locally sourced bird or vegetarian main dish. Opt for native fruits, squash, chutneys, or preserves instead of American sweet potatoes, cranberries, or canned pumpkin to achieve similar flavors and textures.

Invite local friends into the tradition

When celebrating Thanksgiving outside of your home country, it can evolve into a cultural sharing experience rather than just a replica of your familiar traditions. Embrace the holiday's true essence by discussing its complex origins, and encourage guests to contribute a dish or gratitude custom from their own cultural background.

Adapt to smaller kitchens and schedules

City apartments might not have room for big appliances like ovens and refrigerators. Opt for smaller cuts of meat, simple one-pan dishes, make-ahead meals, and potluck items. If you work on Thursdays, consider celebrating on the nearest weekend without feeling tied to the traditional calendar.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need turkey for Thanksgiving abroad?

No, maintain the important traditions and opt for local ingredients for the rest.

How can I explain Thanksgiving to international guests?

Discuss both the contemporary celebration of family and gratitude during Thanksgiving and the intricate colonial and Indigenous history that underlies the holiday in the United States.
