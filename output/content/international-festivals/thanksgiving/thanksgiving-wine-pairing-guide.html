Thanksgiving Wine Pairings: A Flexible Guide

Match Thanksgiving's diverse sweet, savory, and herbal tastes with a range of red, white, and sparkling wines, as well as non-alcoholic options to please all your guests.

Why Thanksgiving is a pairing challenge

Thanksgiving features a harmonious plate of roasted poultry, herbs, butter, tart cranberry, sweet squash, gravy, and dessert. Rather than seeking out a single "perfect" bottle, opt for wines that offer good acidity, moderate alcohol, and enough fruit to complement diverse flavors.

White and sparkling styles

White wines with vibrant acidity are perfect for balancing out rich side dishes. Riesling, Chenin Blanc, Pinot Gris, and lightly oaked Chardonnay are all great options, depending on the sweetness and seasoning of the dish. Sparkling wine is a versatile choice, as its acidity and effervescence can cleanse the palate.

Red styles that stay nimble

Red wines that are lighter to medium-bodied are typically a better match with turkey compared to heavily tannic wines. Varietals such as Pinot Noir, Gamay, and certain Grenache-based wines can enhance the herb and roasted flavors without dominating the dish.

Dessert and nonalcoholic options

When it comes to pumpkin or apple desserts, opt for a wine that is equally sweet or sweeter than the dessert for the best pairing. Nonalcoholic options like sparkling cider, tea, grape-based drinks, and botanical sodas can also be paired based on balancing sweetness, acidity, and spice.

Note: Alcohol is not required; instead, offer considerate nonalcoholic options and serve responsibly.

Frequently asked questions

What wine style is easiest with Thanksgiving?

Bright, food-friendly wines with a nice balance of acidity are known for their versatility. Sparkling wine, Riesling, Pinot Noir, and Gamay are popular choices.

Should every guest drink wine?

Always provide enticing nonalcoholic choices and water. The goal of pairing is to enhance the dining experience, not to force alcohol consumption.
