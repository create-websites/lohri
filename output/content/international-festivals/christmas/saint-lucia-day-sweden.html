Saint Lucia Day in Sweden: Light, Song and Saffron

On December 13th, Lucia celebrations in Sweden are known for their use of light, music, and hospitality, making it one of the country's most iconic winter traditions.

Why Lucia is associated with light

Saint Lucia, a Christian saint, has her feast day celebrated in Scandinavian winter culture with a focus on light during the darkest time of the year. In Sweden, the modern tradition includes a Lucia figure dressed in white with a crown of candles or electric lights.

Today's celebration combines Christian symbolism with local customs, involving schools, workplaces, choirs, and family gatherings, rather than adhering to a single, unaltered medieval ritual.

The procession and songs

Lucia processions commonly feature participants in white robes, star boys, and choirs performing traditional holiday tunes. Various institutions such as schools, churches, businesses, and public spaces may host their own interpretations of the event.

While real candles may still be used in carefully monitored environments, electric candles are frequently chosen for safety reasons, particularly when children are present.

Lussebullar and the food tradition

Lussebullar, also called lussekatter, are traditional saffron buns that are closely associated with the Lucia festival. Often enjoyed with coffee, tea, or other warm seasonal beverages, their vibrant yellow hue and unique curled shape are a visual representation of the holiday.

While ginger biscuits and other holiday treats may be enjoyed alongside the buns, it is the saffron bread that truly stands out as a culinary highlight of the season.

How to celebrate respectfully

To create a cozy home version, try baking saffron buns, playing Swedish Lucia music, dimming the lights, and safely lighting candles. If children join in, emphasize the importance of service, light, and community over perfect costumes.

When participating in a public procession, be sure to adhere to the photography guidelines set by the community and keep in mind that these events hold significance as school, church, or neighborhood traditions, not just tourist attractions.

Context note: Lucia traditions differ throughout Sweden and Swedish-speaking regions, with electric candles being a popular choice for children's processions due to their safety.

Frequently asked questions

When is Saint Lucia Day?

It is observed on December 13.

What are lussebullar?

They are saffron-flavored Swedish buns strongly associated with Lucia season.
