Christmas Main Courses: U.S. and European Traditions

The variety of Christmas main courses reflects the diverse influences of climate, religion, migration, and regional food traditions on holiday tables in Europe and the United States.

Turkey and ham in the United States

Roast turkey is a popular choice for American Christmas dinners, particularly in households that continue the tradition of Thanksgiving-style cooking into December. Baked ham is also a common centerpiece, ideal for serving large groups and complementing make-ahead side dishes.

Regional and immigrant influences can greatly impact the menu, therefore each dish should be considered optional rather than obligatory.

Roast goose and other European meats

In certain regions of Central and Northern Europe, roast goose is strongly linked to Christmas, while in other areas, duck, pork, and game are more commonly seen. These culinary traditions were influenced by historical availability and local farming practices well before the rise of global supermarkets.

Modern families might opt for smaller roasts or streamline traditional menus for practicality.

Carp and seafood traditions

In certain regions of Central and Eastern Europe, Carp is traditionally enjoyed on Christmas Eve. Meanwhile, in Catholic cultures and other areas, meals rich in fish or seafood pay homage to fasting customs observed before Christmas Day.

French Réveillon menus may include oysters, shellfish, and decadent celebratory dishes, yet there is no single standard template.

Building a cross-cultural holiday menu

Opt for a single main dish and a couple of sides that showcase a unique regional narrative instead of overwhelming guests with a plethora of traditional Christmas fare. Take the time to introduce unfamiliar foods to your guests so they can appreciate the story behind each dish on the table.

When blending family heritages, opting for smaller portions of two traditional dishes can hold more significance than completely replacing one.

Context note: Different regions have their own holiday food traditions, and the dishes featured in this guide serve as examples rather than universal standards across the nation.

Frequently asked questions

Is turkey the traditional Christmas meal everywhere?

Various regions have their own traditional foods, such as goose, pork, carp, seafood, and many others.

Why are fish meals common on Christmas Eve?

In certain Christian traditions, they are linked to ancient practices of fasting or refraining from certain foods leading up to Christmas.
