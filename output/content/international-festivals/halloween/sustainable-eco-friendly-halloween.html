Sustainable Spookiness: An Eco-Friendly Halloween Guide

Halloween is a great time to get creative and find eco-friendly ways to minimize waste. Opting for sustainable decorations and costumes that are made from items you already have, can borrow, repair, or reuse is a simple way to do your part for the environment.

Start with what already exists

Instead of purchasing a new costume, consider shopping your closet, exchanging with friends, browsing thrift stores, or getting creative with items from past Halloweens. By using simple items like a black outfit, an old sheet, cardboard, scarves, and accessories, you can easily transform them into multiple recognizable costumes through some styling.

Decorate with reusable and compostable materials

Opt for fabric bunting, paper silhouettes, natural branches, pumpkins, gourds, and reusable lights instead of disposable plastic items. Organize durable decorations in labeled bins to create a yearly collection rather than a one-time purchase.

Make pumpkins part of the food cycle

If a carving pumpkin is safe for consumption, feel free to use the flesh or seeds in recipes; otherwise, follow local composting guidelines. Make sure to remove any candles, paint, glitter, or synthetic decorations before composting.

Rethink treats and energy use

Purchase candy in quantities that match the anticipated number of visitors to minimize excess, add some non-edible treats, and install LED lights with timers. The key to successful sustainability is making it easy enough to continue annually.

Frequently asked questions

Are artificial decorations always worse than natural ones?

Maybe not always. Opting for a long-lasting decoration that can be used repeatedly over the years may be better than constantly purchasing disposable options. The lifespan of the item is important.

Can painted pumpkins be composted?

It varies based on the type of paint and compost regulations in your area. Opt for plain, untreated pumpkin material for the most environmentally-friendly composting option.
