Budget-Friendly Thanksgiving Dinner Planning

Create a budget-friendly Thanksgiving feast with a thoughtful menu, using seasonal ingredients, store-brand items, potluck contributions, and minimizing waste.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingHosting, Prep & Decor

Set a per-person target before shopping

Calculate the total budget for your holiday groceries by dividing it by the number of guests, and make tradeoffs accordingly before heading to the store to avoid overspending on special projects for each dish.

Choose one centerpiece, not five

A turkey, plant-based dish, or other main course can be the focal point of the meal. There's no need for multiple costly proteins. Instead, complement the main dish with affordable seasonal sides like potatoes, squash, carrots, rolls, beans, and easy salads.

Buy ingredients that work in several dishes

Ingredients like onions, celery, herbs, stock, flour, and butter or oil are versatile and can be used in stuffing, gravy, vegetables, and casseroles, helping to reduce waste from half-used packages and forgotten specialty items.

Use potluck logic strategically

To avoid duplicates of the same dish, encourage guests to choose a category to contribute to such as dessert, salad, vegetable side, drinks, or bread instead of providing specific recipes. This will help distribute the cost and workload among everyone.

Include leftover planning in your budget. Utilize containers, sandwiches, soup, and next-day meals to stretch out the holiday grocery bill and create multiple days of meals instead of one large dinner.

Frequently asked questions

Is a whole turkey always the cheapest main?

It's not guaranteed. Consider local prices and the number of guests. Opting for a smaller turkey, turkey breast, plant-based centerpiece, or potluck main dish could be more budget-friendly.

How do I avoid duplicate potluck dishes?

Categorize broadly and maintain a collective list for guests to reference covered topics.
